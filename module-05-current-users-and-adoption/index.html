    Module 5: Current Users and Adoption

    5.1 Institutional Adopters

    BlackRock (BUIDL)

    The BlackRock USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund (BUIDL) is a dominant force in the tokenized treasury market, managing $2.5 billion to $2.9 billion in assets. The fund invests 100% of its assets in short-term U.S. government securities, repurchase agreements, and cash equivalents, maintaining a stable $1.00 value while earning an annual yield of approximately 4.5% that is paid monthly in new tokens. BlackRock leverages Securitize as its transfer agent and tokenization provider, while Bank of New York Mellon (BNY Mellon) serves as the custodian for the underlying cash and securities. Initially launched on Ethereum, BUIDL has expanded its multi-chain strategy to operate natively across 8 blockchains: Aptos, Arbitrum, Avalanche, BNB Chain, Ethereum, Optimism, Polygon, and Solana.

    Franklin Templeton (BENJI)

    The Franklin OnChain U.S. Government Money Fund (FOBXX), represented by the BENJI token, pioneered institutional tokenization when it launched in 2021 as the first U.S.-registered mutual fund to use a public blockchain for transaction processing and share ownership recording. The fund manages approximately $420 million in assets across multiple networks, including Stellar, Polygon, Avalanche, and Aptos. Driving global expansion, Franklin Templeton introduced the first Luxembourg-registered tokenized UCITS money market fund for professional investors in Hong Kong, integrating issuance, distribution, and servicing natively on the blockchain.

    JPMorgan (Kinexys)

    Formerly known as Onyx, JPMorgan rebranded its blockchain and tokenization platform to Kinexys to drive broader mainstream adoption across financial services. This enterprise-grade platform has processed over $1.5 trillion in notional value and handles approximately $2 billion in daily transaction volume. Core components include Kinexys Digital Payments (previously JPM Coin) for instant, real-time multicurrency clearing and settlement, and the Tokenized Collateral Network (TCN). The TCN allows investors to tokenize, move, and utilize traditional assetssuch as money market fundsas collateral for derivatives and repo transactions without requiring the movement of the underlying assets in traditional ledgers.

    KKR

    Private equity giant KKR partnered with Securitize to tokenize a segment of its Health Care Strategic Growth Fund II on the Avalanche public blockchain. This milestone marked one of the first times a major private equity firm offered tokenized fund interests, unlocking institutional-quality alternative investments for a broader pool of accredited investors through a digital interface. This initiative demonstrated how established private equity firms can use blockchain technology to democratize access and streamline historically paper-heavy operations.

    Hamilton Lane

    Managing $835 billion in AUM, Hamilton Lane partnered with Securitize to tokenize three of its funds, covering private equity, private credit, and secondary transactions. By utilizing blockchain technology, the firm drastically lowered its minimum investment thresholds from a typical $5 million down to just $20,000, significantly expanding access for qualified individual investors. Notable examples of this strategy include the tokenized Secondary Fund VI on the Polygon network and the Senior Credit Opportunities (SCOPE) fund, which brings institutional private credit strategies to a wider audience.

    Fidelity (FDIT)

    The Fidelity Digital Interest Token (FDIT) is an Ethereum-based tokenized Treasury fund representing shares of the Fidelity Treasury Digital Fund (FYOXX). Managing over $200 million in assets, FDIT is positioned to aggressively compete with BlackRock's BUIDL by offering a highly attractive 0.20% annual management fee. The fund offers institutional investors 24/7 liquidity, real-time atomic settlements, and seamless integration with decentralized finance (DeFi) ecosystems, and it has already been integrated as a reserve asset for platforms like Ondo Finance.

    5.2 Institutional Adoption Statistics

    Institutional investment in digital assets has reached a significant inflection point, driven by a growing recognition of the asset class's long-term viability and the technological efficiencies of blockchain. The Fidelity Digital Assets Institutional Investor Study highlights robust momentum across global markets, investor segments, and specific product types like tokenized real-world assets (RWAs).

    Overall Institutional Adoption Trends

    Despite periodic market volatility, foundational interest from institutions remains strong. 58% of surveyed institutional investors globally were invested in digital assets in the first half of 2022. Even more notably, 74% of institutions plan to buy or invest in digital assets in the future. This forward-looking commitment indicates that institutional investors are increasingly viewing digital assets not merely as a speculative alternative, but as an independent investment class. Overall, more than half of institutional investors (51%) maintain a positive perception of digital assets, citing high potential upside, innovative technology, and the enablement of decentralization as key appealing features.

    Adoption by Investor Segment

    The depth of digital asset integration varies significantly depending on the risk tolerance and organizational structure of the investor. Digital asset usage is highest among the following segments:

    • Crypto hedge funds and venture capital funds lead with 87% usage, which aligns with their native focus on the industry.

    • High-net-worth (HNW) investors report an 82% usage rate, as these individuals frequently seek portfolio diversification, higher returns, and fractional access to traditionally illiquid markets.

    • Financial advisors show a 73% adoption rate, reflecting growing client demand for digital asset exposure and wealth management solutions that incorporate on-chain products.

    Conversely, more conservative institutional pools of capitalsuch as pensions, defined benefit plans, and endowmentsreport much lower adoption rates. These entities frequently cite price volatility, a lack of clear regulatory frameworks, and security concerns as primary barriers preventing larger capital allocations.

    Geographic Differences in Digital Asset Ownership

    There are distinct geographic variations in how institutions are adopting digital assets, though growth is occurring universally:

    • Asia leads the global market with 69% of institutional investors holding digital assets. Asian institutions also exhibit a stronger appetite for a diverse range of altcoins (such as Bitcoin Cash, USD Coin, and Ripple) alongside Bitcoin and Ethereum.

    • Europe follows closely with a 67% ownership rate. European institutions notably posted an 11-point increase in ownership year-over-year, bolstered by clear regulatory frameworks like the Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation, which provides the legal certainty required for institutions to deploy capital confidently.

    • The United States currently trails at 42% ownership. However, U.S. adoption is accelerating rapidly, seeing a 9-point increase driven largely by HNW investors and growing interest in structured products like digital asset ETFs and tokenized Treasury funds.

    Willingness to Invest in Tokenized RWAs

    Beyond native cryptocurrencies, traditional institutions are highly focused on the tokenization of physical and financial assets. Nearly 60% of institutions express a willingness to invest in tokenized real-world assets (RWAs). This interest is primarily driven by the desire for "on-chain institutional yield"allowing investors to benefit from the speed, 24/7 liquidity, and fractionalization of blockchain efficiency while maintaining the predictable returns and security of traditional finance.

    Within the RWA space, real estate and stocks/equities currently hold the greatest appeal for institutions. Real estate tokenization allows for fractional ownership of high-value, illiquid properties, while automating rental distributions and compliance via smart contracts. Concurrently, tokenized U.S. Treasuries and money market funds (such as BlackRock's BUIDL and Franklin Templeton's BENJI) are capturing massive institutional inflows, serving as a low-risk entry point for traditional firms migrating to on-chain infrastructure.

    5.3 Platform Providers

    Securitize is a leading end-to-end tokenization platform that operates as an SEC-registered broker-dealer, digital transfer agent, fund administrator, and operator of a regulated Alternative Trading System (ATS). It powers high-profile institutional tokenization initiatives, acting as the tokenization provider for BlackRocks $2.9 billion USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund (BUIDL). Securitize also facilitated the tokenization of Hamilton Lane's private equity, private credit, and secondary funds, and tokenized a segment of KKRs Health Care Strategic Growth Fund II on the Avalanche blockchain. Validating its market position, Securitize raised $47 million in a funding round led by BlackRock to expand its real-world asset (RWA) tokenization infrastructure.

    Polymath and Polymesh were early pioneers in digital securities, with Polymath originally driving the development of the ERC-1400 security token standard on Ethereum. Recognizing the limitations of general-purpose networks, they built Polymesh, an institutional-grade Layer 1 (L1) blockchain purpose-built for regulated real-world assets. The Polymesh network embeds identity, compliance, confidentiality, and governance frameworks directly into its base layer to meet the strict regulatory requirements of financial institutions.

    tZERO addresses the critical need for secondary market liquidity by operating as an SEC- and FINRA-regulated broker-dealer and Alternative Trading System (ATS) specifically designed for tokenized securities. It provides vital trading infrastructure, supporting continuous central limit order book (CLOB) trading, block trades, and customizable auction-based liquidity events for digital assets. tZERO integrates with other ecosystem players to create end-to-end pipelines; for example, it partnered with Polymath to allow issuers to mint assets on the Polymesh blockchain and subsequently trade them on tZERO's regulated ATS.

    Tokeny is a prominent European tokenization infrastructure provider and the original creator of the ERC-3643 standard (formerly known as the T-REX protocol). The ERC-3643 standard embeds identity validation (using ONCHAINID) and regulatory compliance directly into the smart contract, ensuring that tokens can only be held and transferred by eligible, KYC/AML-verified investors. Tokeny operates primarily as an enterprise-grade, white-label Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) provider, enabling asset managers to securely issue and manage compliant digital assets without having to build their own infrastructure. Tokeny's framework is highly adopted, having been used to tokenize over $28 billion worth of assets.

    Executive Refresher

    Tier-1 institutions have moved from pilots to production. The tokenization market is no longer an experimental niche; it is a foundational rewrite of financial infrastructure driven by major incumbents like BlackRock, JPMorgan, and Franklin Templeton. These firms are operationalizing blockchain directly within their core balance-sheet infrastructure to achieve tangible utility and scale.

    Treasuries and Money Market Funds (MMFs) dominate current volume. The bedrock of current institutional adoption is low-risk, yield-bearing government debt. Tokenized U.S. Treasuries and MMFs account for over 44% of the real-world asset (RWA) market. Funds like BlackRock's BUIDL and Franklin Templeton's FOBXX manage billions, serving as proof-of-concept for broader market transformation.

    Wealthy individuals are the primary targets for alternative assets. While institutions currently drive MMF tokenization, wealth managers are leveraging the technology to offer fractionalized private equity, private credit, and real estate to High-Net-Worth Individuals (HNWIs). Democratizing access to these traditionally illiquid assets represents a massive $400 billion annual revenue opportunity for the alternatives industry.

    Adoption boasts strong numbers, led by Asia and Europe. 58% of surveyed institutional investors already hold some form of digital assets. Current ownership is highest among institutional investors in Asia (69%) and Europe (67%), compared to the U.S. (42%). However, the U.S. and European markets are experiencing the fastest year-over-year adoption growth rates.

    Tokens are increasingly used as functional collateral. Current institutional users are not simply buying and holding tokens; they are seeking "capital efficiency" through real-time collateral mobility. Tokenized MMFs are actively being pledged as collateral in both decentralized finance (DeFi) and traditional trading environments (like JPMorgan's Kinexys), allowing investors to generate yield while executing seamless, 24/7 transfers.

    Retail participation is expanding but remains gated. Currently, tokenized asset distribution is heavily restricted to institutional and accredited investors due to strict regulatory compliance. However, regulatory frameworks are evolving to permit mainstream retail access. Firms are already securing approvals or filing for retail-approved tokenized funds in jurisdictions like Hong Kong and Singapore.

    Question 10: Cross-Border Wealth Management Platforms and Tokenized Access A global private bank manages high-net-worth clients across 15 jurisdictions. It wants to offer tokenized alternative investment products (private equity, private credit, real estate) to clients in all jurisdictions simultaneously. What is the primary compliance architecture challenge this creates?

    A) Cross-border tokenized offerings are technologically impossible because blockchain networks cannot execute transactions across different national internet jurisdictions.

    B) Each investor's wallet must be compliance-checked against their specific jurisdiction's securities regulations, requiring a multi-jurisdictional investor eligibility registry that automatically enforces different transfer restrictions, accreditation requirements, and offering caps per jurisdiction within the same smart contract.

    C) The bank must issue a separate token for each jurisdiction, making cross-border portfolio construction operationally infeasible for wealth managers.

    D) Cross-border tokenized offerings automatically qualify for a global regulatory exemption since blockchain transactions are technically borderless and therefore outside all national securities laws.

    Answer Key & Explanations

    1. Answer: C) Pensions, endowments, and foundations.

    Explanation: While high-net-worth individuals and crypto hedge funds show the highest adoption rates, pensions, endowments, and foundations have the lowest digital asset adoption but represent the highest AUM among institutional respondents. This indicates that adoption is currently concentrated among lower-AUM investors, leaving massive institutional capital pools as future growth targets for the tokenization industry.

    2. Answer: B) By using public blockchains as primary ledgers to enable 24/7 transferability, real-time atomic settlements, and composability with DeFi platforms for collateral mobility.

    Explanation: BlackRock and Franklin Templeton have driven institutional adoption by utilizing public blockchains (like Ethereum, Stellar, and Polygon) to issue tokenized funds. This architecture allows for real-time, 24/7/365 atomic settlements and seamless integration into DeFi ecosystems, allowing these tokenized funds to be used as yield-bearing collateral in broader financial applications.

    3. Answer: C) North America commands the largest global revenue share, while Asia-Pacific represents the fastest-growing region, driven by proactive regulatory hubs like Singapore and Hong Kong.

    Explanation: In 2025, North America generated the largest regional revenue share (39.10%) in the asset tokenization market, supported by strong venture investment and institutional pilots. Meanwhile, Asia-Pacific is the fastest-growing region (projected 53.75% CAGR through 2031), heavily driven by supportive, structured initiatives in regulatory hubs like Singapore (Project Guardian) and Hong Kong (Fintech 2030).

    4. Answer: D) Creating tokenized feeder funds that drastically lower minimum investment thresholds (e.g., from $5 million to $20,000), thereby expanding access to a broader base of accredited individual investors.

    Explanation: Firms like Hamilton Lane and KKR are pioneering tokenization in private markets to solve structural challenges like high entry barriers. By tokenizing feeder funds, Hamilton Lane successfully reduced minimum investments from the traditional $5 million down to $20,000, effectively democratizing access to institutional-quality private equity and credit for a wider pool of accredited investors.

    5. Answer: A) By embedding compliance mechanisms, identity verification, and transfer restrictions directly into the token's smart contract layer at the protocol level.

    Explanation: To achieve institutional scale on public or permissioned chains, platforms like Securitize rely on compliance-aware token standards (such as ERC-3643 or ERC-1400). These standards enforce jurisdictional rules, KYC/AML identity checks, and transfer restrictions directly within the smart contracts, ensuring that tokens cannot be held or traded by unauthorized parties.

    6. Correct Answer: B Explanation: Sovereign wealth funds typically prioritize capital preservation and regulatory compliance. Tokenized money market funds provide the lowest-risk entry: they offer familiar money market risk characteristics (short-duration, government-backed assets), are managed by regulated institutional managers, and provide on-chain liquidity. This first-step approach allows the SWF to build internal digital asset custody capabilities, develop compliance frameworks for digital assets, and generate proof points before allocating to more complex tokenized alternatives like private equity or real estate.

    7. Correct Answer: B Explanation: Insurance companies face specific inefficiencies in traditional bond markets: T+2 settlement delays affect portfolio rebalancing timing; coupon reinvestment requires manual processing; and collateral transparency relies on periodic custodian reporting. Tokenized bonds solve each: instant settlement improves ALM precision; smart contracts automatically reinvest coupons on schedule; and on-chain transparency allows real-time portfolio monitoring. Several insurance firms (including Zurich Insurance) have begun exploring tokenized bond allocations for precisely these operational improvements.

    8. Correct Answer: B Explanation: Institutional investor surveys consistently identify regulatory uncertainty as the top barrier — unclear classification of tokenized securities, evolving SEC custody rules (the SAB 121 accounting requirement created balance sheet constraints for custodian banks), and jurisdictional fragmentation create compliance risk. Secondary barriers include the operational challenge of building compliant digital asset custody alongside traditional systems, and the internal governance challenge of securing board-level approval for a new asset class. These are solvable institutional barriers, not fundamental technology limitations.

    9. Correct Answer: B Explanation: BNY Mellon's digital asset custody launch is a landmark institutional signal: the world's largest custodian — managing custody for a majority of global institutional investors — treating digital assets as a standard custody asset class removes a fundamental operational barrier. Institutional investors who previously cited 'no trusted custodian' as a barrier can now hold tokenized assets within existing BNY Mellon relationships, using familiar legal agreements, operational workflows, and regulatory reporting frameworks. This accelerates adoption by embedding digital assets into mainstream institutional infrastructure.

    10. Correct Answer: B Explanation: Cross-border tokenized offerings require compliance-aware token standards (ERC-3643 or ERC-1400) that integrate multi-jurisdictional identity registries. When an investor in Singapore, the UAE, or Germany attempts to purchase or transfer a token, the smart contract must automatically check that investor's jurisdiction-specific eligibility: EU accreditation standards differ from U.S. Reg D requirements, which differ from Singapore's MAS rules. The T-REX protocol and similar solutions enable a single token smart contract to enforce jurisdiction-specific transfer rules per investor wallet, enabling global distribution with localized compliance.
