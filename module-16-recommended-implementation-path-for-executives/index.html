    Module 16: Recommended Implementation Path for Executives

    16.1 Implementation Paths by Institution Type

    Asset Managers: Start with Tokenized MMFs and Partner with Established Platforms For asset managers, the recommended implementation path begins with tokenizing money market funds (MMFs) as the lowest-risk entry point. Tokenized MMFs have already seen immense success, with products like BlackRock’s BUIDL and Franklin Templeton’s FOBXX (BENJI) demonstrating that blockchain-integrated technology can seamlessly process transactions, record share ownership, and offer 24/7 liquidity.

    Rather than building proprietary infrastructure from scratch, asset managers should partner with established tokenization providers like Securitize or Polymath. * Securitize is a market leader, operating as an SEC-registered broker-dealer, transfer agent, and Alternative Trading System (ATS). It has already powered tokenized funds for top-tier managers like BlackRock, Hamilton Lane, and KKR. * Polymath (and its Polymesh blockchain) offers an institutional-grade, purpose-built Layer-1 network for regulated real-world assets, embedding identity and compliance directly into the token level.

    Once operational confidence is established with MMFs, asset managers can expand into more complex private market funds.

    Private Equity (PE) Firms: Tokenized Feeder Funds and Lowering Minimums PE firms face structural challenges like illiquidity, 7-10 year lockup periods, and high minimum investments (typically $1 million to $5 million). The recommended path for PE firms is to launch tokenized feeder funds that tie into existing flagship funds. This allows them to maintain their traditional General Partner-Limited Partner (GP-LP) structures while adding a tokenized access layer.

    By utilizing tokenization, PE firms can enable fractional ownership and reduce minimum investment requirements to the $20,000–$50,000 range. This strategy effectively democratizes access, allowing firms to test broader investor appetite among high-net-worth individuals and retail investors. A prime example of this success is Hamilton Lane, which partnered with Securitize to tokenize a secondary fund, reducing its minimum investment from $5 million to just $20,000.

    Banks: Developing Blockchain Payment Infrastructure and the Kinexys Model Banks should focus on developing foundational blockchain payment and settlement infrastructure to serve as the backbone of the tokenized economy. The model to emulate is JPMorgan's Kinexys (formerly Onyx), which is one of the world's most established bank-operated blockchains.

    The Kinexys platform processes approximately $2 billion per day and has handled over $1.5 trillion in notional value since its launch. By following this model, banks can: * Offer tokenization services to clients as a revenue-generating product line. * Create tokenized collateral networks that enable the efficient transfer of collateral ownership without moving assets in underlying ledgers, thereby streamlining repo and derivatives markets. * Facilitate real-time, cross-border tokenized deposits and multi-currency clearing using digital payment systems like JPM Coin (now Kinexys Digital Payments).

    Real Estate Firms: Single Property Proof-of-Concept Real estate is a massive but highly illiquid asset class characterized by high capital requirements and lengthy transaction times. For real estate firms, the recommended path is to begin with a single high-quality commercial property tokenization as a proof-of-concept.

    Starting with a single property allows firms to structure clear governance for fractional ownership and test the operational workflows. It enables them to provide transparent reporting on operations, financials, and valuations directly via the blockchain before attempting to scale to a full portfolio approach. Once proven, this model allows properties worth millions to be fractionalized into affordable tokens (e.g., $10,000 chunks), automating rental income distribution via smart contracts and enabling secondary-market liquidity. A historical blueprint is Elevated Returns, which successfully tokenized $18 million worth of the Aspen Ridge Resort in 2018.

    Institutional Investors: Allocating to Established Funds and Building Capabilities For institutional investors, the journey into tokenization does not require minting their own assets immediately. Instead, the recommended path is to allocate capital to established tokenized funds managed by major, trusted players like BlackRock, Franklin Templeton, or Fidelity.

    Simultaneously, institutional investors must build their internal operational capabilities for holding, safeguarding, and transacting in digital assets. This includes: * Integrating with institutional-grade digital asset custodians (such as Fireblocks, Anchorage Digital, or BNY Mellon) that offer secure Multi-Party Computation (MPC) wallet infrastructure and robust policy enforcement. * Evaluating direct participation in tokenization consortiums, such as the Canton Network, to safely interact with other financial institutions. * Considering the tokenization of highly illiquid positions within their existing portfolios to unlock capital efficiency and create secondary liquidity.

    Executive Refresher

    Here is a concise executive refresher summary outlining the recommended implementation path for tokenization initiatives:

    1. Start Small with a Targeted Pilot Avoid attempting to overhaul existing legacy systems all at once. Begin with a low-risk, high-value pilot—such as tokenizing a portion of a money market fund, a private equity feeder fund, or a single high-quality commercial property. Establish clear proof points in a 90-to-180-day window to demonstrate tangible benefits, such as streamlined distribution or faster capital calls, before scaling.

    2. Assemble a Cross-Functional Center of Excellence (CoE) Tokenization is not solely an IT project; it requires a holistic approach. Build a dedicated cross-functional team encompassing legal (securities law), compliance, finance, operations, and technology. Invest in upskilling client-facing staff and leadership so they understand blockchain fundamentals and can confidently navigate client conversations and operational shifts.

    3. Embed "Compliance by Design" from Day One Regulatory adherence is non-negotiable and must be built into your architecture. Engage legal counsel early to select appropriate jurisdictions and ensure your technology incorporates automated compliance. Utilize compliance-aware token standards (like ERC-3643 or ERC-1400) to hardcode KYC/AML checks, investor eligibility, and transfer restrictions directly into the smart contracts.

    4. Separate the Asset, the Legal Rights, and the Technology A critical rule for executives is to never confuse the token with the asset itself. The technology only defines automation and speed, not legality. You must ensure that the digital token is explicitly tied to a legally enforceable wrapper (like an SPV) and that the rights, obligations, and ownership claims of the underlying real-world asset are clearly documented.

    5. Prioritize Interoperability and Strategic Partnerships Do not build proprietary, siloed infrastructure that locks your assets in. Leverage established, institutional-grade vendors for tokenization platforms (e.g., Securitize, Tokeny) and digital asset custody (e.g., Fireblocks). Ensure your systems rely on global standards that allow for seamless cross-chain interoperability and can integrate with traditional financial market infrastructures (FMIs) and legacy systems.

    6. Define Success with Clear, Measurable KPIs Before launching, outline exactly what pain points the tokenization initiative aims to solve and define success with concrete milestones. Evaluate your pilot based on specific metrics such as investor onboarding speed, reduction in minimum investment thresholds, secondary transfer execution success, and operational cost savings. Use these metrics to determine your criteria for expanding to new asset classes or broader investor bases.
